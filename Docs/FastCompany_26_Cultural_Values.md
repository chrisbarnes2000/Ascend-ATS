# Fast Company Impact Council: 26 Cultural Values Companies Get Wrong

*Source: Fast Company Impact Council (Published: 09-04-2026)*  
*URL: https://www.fastcompany.com/91598368/26-cultural-values-companies-get-wrong*

---

## Overview

Organizational culture is often defined by its values, or lack thereof. Values are a key ingredient in “what’s it like to work here” that potential hires try to discover before signing on. It’s also what causes current employees, clients, or customers to stay or leave.

Values often get defined in mission statements or corporate branding. But stating what they are doesn’t always make them so. Members of the Fast Company Impact Council shared 26 cultural values that companies frequently get wrong and how to fix them.

---

## The 26 Cultural Values

### 1. ORIGINALITY
> Most companies get originality wrong: They confuse relevance with resemblance, assuming brands and products should look, sound, or behave “a certain way.” In established categories, teams often copy successful competitors until their brand becomes interchangeable. The antidote is thoughtful questioning: *“What can we credibly contribute that others cannot?”* and *“What non-consensus belief do we hold about this category?”*  
— **Erik Herrström, STUDIO HERRSTRÖM**

### 2. EXCELLENCE AND EMPATHY
> Too many organizations treat excellence and empathy as competing priorities. High performance is built on “gracious professionalism”—respect and kindness make excellence possible. When people feel valued and empowered, they become better problem-solvers and stronger collaborators.  
— **Chris Moore, FIRST**

### 3. COMMUNITY-ENGAGEMENT
> Companies that treat community engagement as a one-way ask burn out donors, volunteers, and partners. The stronger model runs both ways: offering durable value back like training, mentorship, or a real seat in decision-making. Reciprocity builds true partnership.  
— **Kellie Lauth, MindSpark**

### 4. INNOVATION
> Innovation is not a department, campaign, or tech investment—it is a behavior. It's about creating a culture where people are allowed to question the old way, test new ideas, and learn without fear of looking unfinished. Space must be made for curiosity, trust, and intelligent risk-taking.  
— **Virtyt Pula, TOML Collective**

### 5. TRANSPARENCY
> Real transparency isn’t the well-crafted update; it’s what happens when the honest answer is harder than the polished one. Organizations that get it right treat transparency as a cultural commitment, not a tool deployed selectively. Honest communication compounds trust and engagement.  
— **Meredith Rosenberg, NU Advisory Partners**

### 6. A SENSE OF BELONGING
> Belonging requires inclusive action and shared experiences. Outreach and giving programs (such as paid days of service) allow employees to participate, building community both internally and externally.  
— **Mitch Smith, MG2**

### 7. STEWARDSHIP
> Stewardship recognizes leadership as temporary and that consequences outlast tenure. It’s not just whether you won this quarter, but whether you left the brand, team, and trust stronger than you found them.  
— **R. Ethan Braden, Texas A&M University**

### 8. CULTURAL FIT IN HIRING
> Companies hire for “cultural fit” when they should hire **culture builders** (culture add). “Fit” implies conformity. Hire people who add to your culture, challenge assumptions, and bring missing lived experiences. Proximity to the communities served changes what gets built.  
— **Nathan Friedman, Understood.org**

### 9. LEARNING AND TRANSPARENCY
> Companies over-index on immediate results at the expense of long-term learning agility. Furthermore, transparency means sharing what helps people connect the dots to do their jobs better—not dumping unrefined information that causes paralysis or anxiety.  
— **Tami Rosen, Executive board and advisor**

### 10. ACCOUNTABILITY
> Accountability often slips into finding fault. In reality, it’s about creating space where raising a small problem early is welcomed rather than penalized. When honesty isn't penalized, people speak up sooner.  
— **Denas Grybauskas, Oxylabs**

### 11. PEOPLE FIRST
> "People first" fails when prioritized in the wrong sequence. The healthiest ordering starts with: (1) Members/Users served, (2) Paying Clients/Employers, (3) Employees delivering care/service, (4) Surrounding Communities, and (5) Investors—who benefit naturally when the first four are taken care of.  
— **Glen Tullman, Transcarent**

### 12. PURPOSE
> Purpose that only shows up in communications is a marketing line. True purpose acts as a structured mechanism and filter for hard decisions under pressure, capital allocation, and tough trade-offs.  
— **Michele Walsh, UNICEF USA**

### 13. MOVING FAST
> Moving fast demands more discipline, not less. Pacing requires listening to feedback post-shipment so teams actually learn, improve, and provide durable real-world value.  
— **Minna Song, EliseAI**

### 14. COLLABORATION
> Collaboration is not a poster on the wall. It comes to life when organizations build structures, systems, and processes that institutionalize co-creation and hold teams accountable for living it daily.  
— **Maril Gagen MacDonald, Gagen MacDonald**

### 15. WORK HARD ALL DAY LONG
> Working hard non-stop is a myth and counterproductive. Orientation to the work (feeling good, positive attitude, finding enjoyment in the task) matters more than willingness to struggle continuously.  
— **Lindsey Witmer Collins, WLCM App Studio**

### 16. INNOVATION TALENT
> Containing innovation to a specific title or department limits the organization. Innovation talent should raise the capability and agency of the entire company, equipping everyone with tools to test ideas continuously.  
— **Patrick Frend, Delve**

### 17. CURIOSITY
> Companies say they value expertise when they really reward certainty. Great cultures make room to keep asking questions, challenge assumptions, and admit when obvious answers aren't the whole answer.  
— **Gail Zauder, AbleFly Inc**

### 18. CREATIVITY
> Sorting employees into "creatives" and "non-creatives" limits contributions. Creativity must be actively nurtured across all roles (analysts, managers, sales) as a shared capacity.  
— **Maurice Twomey, Roto Group, LLC**

### 19. PHILANTHROPY
> Philanthropy is often under-prioritized or misunderstood. Building a culture of giving back helps attract top talent, retain customers, and capture new business because people prefer companies that value humanity alongside profit.  
— **Ed Mitzen, Business For Good Foundation**

### 20. TEAM AS "A FAMILY"
> Calling a team "a family" can be misleading and uncomfortable. Teams are an **ecosystem**—interconnected with unique functions and strengths. Culture is built on mutual respect, interdependence, and shared purpose, not forced familial bonds.  
— **Edgar Villanueva, Decolonizing Wealth Project**

### 21. THE POWER OF CULTURE
> Employees shape the brand every day through their interactions. Aligning culture with core values strengthens customer relationships, brand promises, employee engagement, and authentic reputation.  
— **Chris K Bailey, Bailey Brand Consulting**

### 22. INCLUSION
> Inclusion stops being effective if it's just who is in the room. True inclusion embraces cognitive diversity, communication styles, lived experience, and allows different perspectives to influence actual outcomes.  
— **Susan Watts, SPACECRAFT**

### 23. MISSION
> A mission isn't text on a wall; it's a decision filter. When every product, hiring, and partnership decision is weighed against stated purpose—especially decisions no one sees—mission becomes operational.  
— **Cody Barbo, Trust & Will**

### 24. KINDNESS
> "Nice" vs "Kind": Being *nice* avoids conflict and preserves comfort now. Being *kind* serves people and the mission by sharing hard truths respectfully, setting clear expectations, and making fair decisions. Kindness builds long-term trust.  
— **Hala Hanna, MIT Solve**

### 25. HUSTLE CULTURE
> Burnout is not a default requirement. Endurance requires pacing and rest. Operating in competitive markets requires rest and recovery—all-in full-out should not be the baseline.  
— **Eric Simons, Bolt.new**

### 26. SOCIAL IMPACT
> Empowering employees as a collective force for good (matching donations, cause competitions, paid volunteer PTO) builds organizational alignment and community value.  
— **Jaymes Black, The Trevor Project**

## Application to Ascend ATS & RapportVerse

This reference document guides the implementation of **Culture-Add Interviewing & Values Alignment** across both Job Seekers and Employers in Ascend ATS:

1. **Job Seekers**:
   - Evaluate candidate non-consensus beliefs ("What can you credibly contribute?").
   - Target workplace culture alignment (e.g., Gracious Professionalism, Culture-Add over Fit, Pacing/Rest vs Burnout).
   - Behavioral interview prep using the 26 Cultural Values as STAR question anchors.

2. **Employers & Recruiters**:
   - Requisition cultural filter tags (Culture Add criteria, Decision Filters, Ecosystem vs Family).
   - Structured candidate interview rubrics assessing Cognitive Diversity, Psychological Safety, and Kindness vs Niceness.
   - Transparency audit for job requisitions to ensure mission alignment.

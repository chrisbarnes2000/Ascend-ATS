*Source: Manpower Insights Blog*

## Overview

Working with a recruiter allows job seekers to expand search resources, access the hidden job market, and accelerate career progression. Recruiters often possess deep insight into hiring managers’ needs, workplace culture, and upcoming unposted roles. When treated as a professional partnership, a recruiter becomes one of a job seeker's strongest career allies.

---

## What Does a Recruiter Do?

A recruiter connects qualified candidates with employers needing specific skills. Good recruiters never charge job seekers—they are paid by the employer.

Effective recruiters must understand:
1. **The Employer Side**: Job requirements, expectations, and workplace culture.
2. **The Candidate Side**: Skills, experience, career goals, and preferences.

Recruiters also:
- Refine resumes for specific roles
- Prepare candidates for interviews
- Share candid feedback from hiring managers
- Advocate for candidates during salary and offer discussions

---

## Finding the Right Recruiter

1. **Look for Specialization**: Focus on recruiters dedicated to specific industries or roles (IT, engineering, healthcare, finance).
2. **Research Reputation**: Check LinkedIn, company credentials, and engagement.
3. **Ask Thoughtful Questions**: Ensure the recruiter asks about long-term goals rather than pushing fast placements.
4. **Avoid Red Flags**: Watch out for pressure to accept mismatched roles or requests for payment.
5. **Leverage Your Network**: Referrals from colleagues and professional groups.

## The 5 Essential Keys to Working with a Recruiter

### 1. Make a Great Impression
- First impressions dictate how recruiters market you to hiring managers.
- Meet face-to-face or via video when possible; dress professionally; articulate experience and goals clearly.
- Maintain prompt, respectful, and positive communication—attitude and reliability directly impact recruiter advocacy confidence.

### 2. Build Your Relationship on Trust and Clear Communication
- Be transparent about qualifications, career gaps, and expectations.
- Clearly state salary expectations, commute/location limits, remote/hybrid preferences, and culture needs early.
- Transparency prevents wasted time and ensures true alignment.

### 3. Follow Up Often
- Recruiters manage multiple candidate pipelines simultaneously; proactive follow-up keeps you top of mind.
- Debrief quickly post-interview (share impression and feedback).
- Keep recruiters informed if you are interviewing elsewhere or receive independent offers—this enhances their leverage when advocating and negotiating for you.

### 4. Cooperation Is Critical
- Treat recruitment as a team effort with agreed timelines and next steps.
- Leverage recruiter expertise: resume positioning advice, interview coaching, and insight into hiring manager expectations.

### 5. Stay in Touch Beyond the Job Search
- Maintain long-term relationships post-placement.
- Update recruiters when earning promotions, certifications, or new skills.
- Keeps you top-of-mind for future career opportunities.
